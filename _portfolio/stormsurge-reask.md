## Overview
Tropical cyclones are one of the most damaging natural catastrophes affecting the Philippines and Hong Kong -- bringing not only destructive winds, but also heavy precipitation and storm surge that can devastate coastal communities. This project evaluates the relationship between tropical cyclone wind and storm surge risk from an insurance industry perspective, providing a stochastic inundation database for catastrophe risk assessment.

## Methods
A subset of 10,000 synthetic tropical cyclone events per region was selected from a larger event set based on their potential to generate significant storm surges, using the Integrated Kinetic Energy (IKE) index as a ranking metric. High-resolution storm surge inundation was then simulated at up to 30 m resolution across all coastal area using [SCHISM](https://ccrm.vims.edu/schismweb/), covering all selected events. The resulting inundation footprints were used for insured loss estimation against client exposure portfolios.

## My role

My main contributions include designing the modelling framework and running over 20,000 high-resolution storm surge simulations, and delivering a production-ready inundation footprints database for client use.
